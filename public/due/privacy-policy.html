Privacy Policy

Due — Bills, Save, Spend  ·  Effective 16 August 2026

Due keeps your money on your phone. Your bills, income, savings and balances are stored in a database on the device itself. There is no Due account, no Due server, and no copy of your finances anywhere we can reach.

We do not sell your data. We do not show ads. We do not track you.

Two things do leave your phone, and only when you choose to use them: voice commands and subscription payments. Both are handled by Google, not by us. Both are explained below.

This policy describes how the Due mobile application (com.aliamz.due), published by Aliamz Digital of Cagayan de Oro City, Philippines, handles your information.

What Due stores on your device

All of the following is written to a private database inside Due's own storage area, which no other app can read:

  • Bills — name, amount, due date and how often they repeat
  • Income — amount, and the schedule you are paid on
  • Savings — amounts you have set aside, and whether they are protected
  • Money movements — the record of what was paid, saved or spent, which is what every balance in the app is calculated from
  • Settings — your reminder time, chosen reminder sound or voice, and reminder wording

Where you have enabled spoken reminders, the sentence to be read out — which may contain a bill's name and amount — is also stored in Due's private app-preferences file, so the reminder can still be spoken when Due is not running. That file is likewise readable only by Due.

None of this is transmitted anywhere. Due contains no networking code: it does not upload, back up or sync your financial information, and it cannot, because there is nowhere for it to go.

What leaves your device

Voice commands

If you use the voice assistant, Due passes your speech to Android's speech recognition service. On most Android phones this means the audio is sent to Google for transcription and is subject to Google's Privacy Policy and to your own Google account's voice and activity settings. Some devices transcribe on the device instead — that is decided by your phone and your Google settings, not by Due.

Due sends only your speech. It does not attach your balances, bills, income or transaction history to that request. The text that comes back is interpreted on the phone, and the recording is not kept by Due.

If you would rather nothing left the device, do not use the voice assistant. Every action it performs can be done by tapping instead, and Due works fully without ever granting microphone access.

Subscription payments (Due+)

Due+ is sold through Google Play Billing. Google handles the entire payment and holds your payment details — Due never sees, receives or stores your card, bank or billing information.

What Due receives back from Google is limited to whether a valid subscription exists and an identifier for the transaction, which is used only to unlock Due+ features on your device. Payments are covered by Google Play's Terms of Service and Google's Privacy Policy.

What Due does not do

  • No account, sign-up, email address or phone number is required or collected
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or usage tracking of any kind
  • No third-party analytics or marketing SDKs
  • No selling, renting or sharing of personal information with anyone
  • No sending of your financial data to any artificial-intelligence service

Permissions Due asks for, and why

Keeping your data, and deleting it

Your data stays on your phone until you remove it. You can delete individual bills, income or savings inside the app at any time.

Uninstalling Due deletes all of it. Because there is no server copy, that is final — we cannot recover your data for you, and nobody else can obtain it from us, because we never had it. Due can write a backup file first, so moving to a new phone does not have to mean starting over.

About backup files. A backup contains everything listed above, saved as plain readable text. It is not encrypted, and Due does not send it anywhere or know where you put it — the file is yours. But it holds your finances in full, so treat it the way you would a bank statement: if you save it to a cloud drive or email it to yourself, your financial details go with it.

If your phone has Android's automatic app backup enabled, your device may include Due's data in your own Google backup. That backup belongs to you and your Google account; it is governed by Google's Privacy Policy, and you can turn it off in your phone's system settings.

Children

Due is a personal-finance tool intended for adults and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect information from children.

Your rights

Because Due holds no personal data on any server, there is no account to access, export or erase, and no request you need to send us to be forgotten — deleting the app does it immediately and completely.

Under the Philippines' Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173), and under equivalent laws elsewhere, you retain your rights over your personal information. If you believe Due has handled your information improperly, please contact us using the details below.

Changes to this policy

If Due ever begins collecting information it does not collect today, this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the effective date at the top will change. Material changes will also be described in the app's release notes on Google Play.
